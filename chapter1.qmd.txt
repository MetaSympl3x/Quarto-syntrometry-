# Introduction & Methodological Architecture {.unnumbered}

::: {.callout-important}
## The Master Epistemic Firewall Non-Transfer Principle

Every mathematical deduction, physical hypothesis, and empirical comparison is governed by the strict non-transfer of justificatory warrant across epistemic strata:

$$\mathbf{A}\text{-Mathematical Validity} \;\not\Rightarrow_{\text{arch}}\; \mathbf{C}\text{-Physical Hypothesis} \;\not\Rightarrow_{\text{arch}}\; \mathbf{E}\text{-Empirical Confirmation}$$

$$\text{A formal proof in Stratum } \mathbf{A} \text{ does not, by itself, validate a physical realization in Stratum } \mathbf{C}.$$

Formal derivability ($\Gamma_A \vdash_{\mathcal{F}} c$) certifies internal consistency within declared axioms; it does not substitute for empirical testing, nor does experimental adequacy transfer historical intent to heuristic explicanda ($\mathbf{D}$).
:::

The intellectual edifice of Burkhard Heim’s *Syntrometrische Maximentelezentrik* (SM, 2000, pp. 6–23) does not commence with the unexamined assumption of an objective, pre-given external reality, nor does it begin with the dogmatic postulation of mechanical spacetime axioms [@heim2000]. Instead, Heim initiates his foundational treatise with an uncompromising epistemological critique of what he designates as the *anthropomorphe Transzendentalästhetik* (anthropomorphic transcendental aesthetics, SM, p. 6) [@heim2000]. Drawing upon and simultaneously radicalizing the Kantian critical tradition, Heim argues that throughout the history of Western natural philosophy, physical modeling has suffered from a profound category mistake: the pervasive, unconscious human tendency to mistake the contingent sensory apparatus, neural filtering mechanisms, and cognitive habits of our biological species for the intrinsic, ontological structure of reality itself.

## The Crisis of Bivalent Reductionism {.unnumbered}

Historically, formal scientific inquiry has operated almost exclusively within the confines of an unyielding, two-valued logic (*zweideutige Logik*, SM, p. 5). In this classical Boolean paradigm, every conceivable assertion is forcefully cleaved into the discrete binary categories of absolute truth ($1$) or absolute falsehood ($0$). When this bivalent, discrete perceptual apparatus is directed toward the primordial ground of existence (*Urerfahrung der Existenz*, SM, p. 7), it inevitably fractures continuous, context-dependent natural phenomena into irreconcilable antinomies (*Antagonismen*, SM, p. 6) [@heim2000].

These antagonisms—such as the apparent contradiction between continuous wave fields and localized discrete particles in quantum mechanics, or the tension between objective mechanical determinism and subjective intentional agency—are not inherent flaws in the fabric of nature. Rather, they are structural projection artifacts arising from a fundamental categorical mismatch: the attempt to compress a continuous, multi-dimensional, context-sensitive reality into a rigid, zero-dimensional, bivalent cognitive container.

## Reflexive Abstraction as the Epistemic Engine {.unnumbered}

To transcend these cognitive limitations without succumbing to unanchored subjective relativism or postmodern nihilism, Heim formulates the core methodological engine of his entire system: **reflexive Abstraktion** (reflexive abstraction, SM, p. 6) [@heim2000]. Rather than pretending that the human observer can be magically excised from the act of observation, reflexive abstraction systematically turns inward to analyze the relational structure of cognition itself. It abstracts away from idiosyncratic physiological sensory channels in order to isolate invariant relational elements (**Konnexreflexionen**) whose structural integrity is evaluated across systematically defined, context-dependent **subjektiven Aspekten** ($S$).

From the vantage point of contemporary mathematical philosophy, Heim's program foreshadows the discovery that logic is not an absolute, static monolith, but an internal geometric feature of the category of observation. In modern category theory, this insight is embodied in the concept of a **Grothendieck Topos**: a mathematical universe where geometric spaces and logical deduction merge into a single entity, and where truth values vary continuously according to local context.

Within a framework $U$, internal questions of assertion, consistency, and implication are governed by Kripke–Joyal sheaf forcing $U \Vdash \phi$ over the subobject classifier Heyting algebra $\Omega(U)$. # Stratum D: Historical Exegesis of the Subjective Aspect (SM, pp. 6–23)

::: {.callout-tip}
## Historical Vignette: The Laboratory of the Mind: Burkhard Heim's Solitary Path
In 1944, a laboratory accident left Burkhard Heim profoundly disabled. His subsequent work transformed severe sensory isolation into a disciplined practice of visual and relational mathematical reasoning. This historical vignette motivates, but does not prove, the reconstruction's emphasis on reflexive abstraction.
:::

In Heim's original philosophical architecture, a subjective aspect ($S$) does not represent a passive window onto an unmediated world. Rather, an aspect constitutes an active epistemic filter determined by *"die Form und dem Umfang der ihm zugehörigen Reflexionsmöglichkeiten"* [@heim2000] (the form and scope of its associated possibilities of reflection, SM, p. 8). Rather than treating a perspective as an unstructured point in an abstract state space, Heim deconstructs the subjective aspect into three coupled, interdependent structural components: the descriptive statement potential (**Prädikatrix**), the qualifying subjective attitude (**Dialektik**), and the active channel linkage that binds them into a unified judgment (**Koordination**).

## Die Prädikatrix ($P_n$) and Prädikatbänder: Continuous Semantic Ranges

In classical formal logic, predication is treated as an instantaneous, binary assignment: an entity either satisfies a predicate or fails it, mapping deterministically into the discrete set $\{0, 1\}$. Heim’s foundational departure begins with the observation that human cognitive experience never encounters descriptive properties in such sterile isolation. When a conscious subject judges an observed phenomenon to be *warm*, *rapid*, or *dense*, the judgment does not designate a single mathematical point on the real line, but a contextual interval of sensory and semantic tolerance.

To capture this experiential reality, Heim replaces the static atomic predicate with the **Prädikatband** (predicate band). The totality of possible descriptive predicates available to an aspect is organized into the Prädikatrix, $P_n \equiv [f_q]_n$, where $q \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ indexes $n$ distinct property channels (SM, p. 8) [@heim2000]. Each predicate band is formalized as an ordered triple:

$$f_q \equiv \begin{pmatrix} a \\ f \\ b \end{pmatrix}_q, \quad P_n \equiv \begin{bmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ f \\ b \end{pmatrix}_q \end{bmatrix}_n \quad (1 \le q \le n) \tag{SM-1}$$

In this formulation, $a_q \in \mathbb{R}$ represents the lower semantic threshold below which the property is imperceptible or inapplicable, $b_q \in \mathbb{R}$ denotes the upper saturation limit beyond which the property ceases to provide descriptive differentiation, and $f_q \in [a_q, b_q]$ represents the focal evaluation within that active range. Classical bivalent logic is thus revealed to be an extreme, degenerate idealization—the singular limit where the semantic bandwidth collapses to a single point ($a_q = b_q = f_q$).

Crucially, these bands do not exist in an unorganized heap. The subjective mind actively imposes an evaluative hierarchy through the **prädikative Basischiffre** ($z_n$), described by Heim as a *"Bezugssystem der prädikativen Wertrelationen"* [@heim2000] (a reference system of predicative value relations, SM, p. 9). The cipher acts as a cognitive lens: it dictates which properties take perceptual precedence in the current mental state, establishes their sequential priority, and determines the orientation—the *Sinn des Intervalls*—governing whether an increase in the parameter represents an approach toward or a departure from the subject's evaluative baseline. The evaluated predicatrix is denoted $P_{nn} \equiv z_n ; P_n$. Permutation operators $C$ (which reorder the sequence of bands) and $c$ (which flip the orientation of individual intervals) combine into $C' = c ; C$, modeling qualitative shifts in the subject's descriptive framing.

## Die Dialektik ($D_n$) and Diatropenbänder: The Structure of Subjective Nuance

Heim emphasizes that an assertion is never purely descriptive; it is inextricably colored by the subject’s internal orientation, emotional valence, degree of certainty, and intentional stance (*"es liegt in der Natur des Subjektiven selbst..."*, SM, p. 9) [@heim2000]. A measurement reported with absolute scientific conviction possesses a vastly different epistemic and cognitive function than the same measurement reported as a hesitant conjecture or an alarming anomaly.

Heim formalizes this qualifying dimension through the **Dialektik** $D_n \equiv [d_q]_n$, composed of $n$ **Diatropenbänder** (diatrope bands):

$$d_q \equiv \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ d \\ \beta \end{pmatrix}_q, \quad D_n \equiv \begin{bmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ d \\ \beta \end{pmatrix}_q \end{bmatrix}_n \tag{SM-2}$$

Here, $\alpha_q$ and $\beta_q$ represent the boundary limits of a qualitative modifier (such as the range from complete doubt to absolute certainty, or from strong aversion to intense desirability), while $d_q \in [\alpha_q, \beta_q]$ represents the active focal qualification. Analogous to the predicatrix, a **dialektische Basischiffre** ($\zeta_n$) establishes the priority and directional polarity of these diatropic nuances, yielding the evaluated dialectic $D_{nn} \equiv \zeta_n ; D_n$. Transformation operators $\Gamma'$ act upon $\zeta_n$ to model internal psychological or epistemological realignments.

## Die Koordination ($K_n$) and the Complete Aspect Schema ($S$)

Neither raw descriptive statements ($P_{nn}$) nor isolated subjective qualifications ($D_{nn}$) possess autonomous assertion value in Heim's epistemology: *"Weder die Diatropen noch die Prädikate besitzen für sich allein Aussagewert, sondern müssen derart koordiniert werden, daß jedes Diatrop ein Prädikat prägt"* [@heim2000] (Neither diatropes nor predicates possess assertion value on their own; rather, they must be coordinated in such a way that each diatrope imprints a predicate, SM, p. 10). A qualification without a predicate is empty emotion; a predicate without a diatrope is unperceived data.

This indispensable synthesis is executed by the **Koordination** ($K_n$), also termed the *Korrespondenzschema*:

$$K_n \equiv E_n F(\zeta_n, z_n) \tag{SM-3}$$

The coordination operates on two levels:

1. **Chiffrenkoordination ($F(\zeta_n, z_n)$):** A functional alignment that correlates the evaluative priority system of the qualifiers ($\zeta_n$) with that of the descriptive properties ($z_n$). It ensures that the subject's emotional or cognitive focus is applied to the appropriate descriptive channels.
2. **Koordinationsbänder ($E_n \equiv [\chi_q]_n \equiv [(y \, \chi \, r)_q^T]_n$):** A schema of $n$ coordination bands that enforce the specific mathematical coupling rules (*Prägung*). Each band $\chi_q$ binds the $q$-th evaluated diatrope to the $q$-th evaluated predicate.

The synthesized totality of these structures constitutes the complete **Subjektiver Aspekt ($S$)** (SM, p. 10, Eq. 1):

$$S \equiv \left[ D_{nn} \times K_n \times P_{nn} \right] \equiv \left[ \zeta_n ; \begin{bmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ d \\ \beta \end{pmatrix}_q \end{bmatrix}_n \times \begin{bmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} y \\ \chi \\ r \end{pmatrix}_q \end{bmatrix}_n F(\zeta_n, z_n) \times z_n ; \begin{bmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ f \\ b \end{pmatrix}_q \end{bmatrix}_n \right] \tag{SM-4}$$

The schema $S$ represents a complete, internally unified subjective viewpoint. It defines the entire universe of statements that can be meaningfully formulated and evaluated from within that specific cognitive orientation [@heim2000].

## Aspektivsysteme ($\mathfrak{A}$), Metropie ($g$), and Aspektivfelder

Subjective aspects do not float in disconnected isolation. A conscious subject constantly shifts focus, recalibrates emotional stances, and refines descriptive observations. Heim models these dynamic transitions through transformation operators termed **Systemgeneratoren** ($\alpha$) (SM, p. 11) [@heim2000]. When an initial aspect $S_0$ is acted upon by a $p$-valued generator $\alpha$, it unfolds a manifold of $p^m$ related aspects, forming an **Aspektivsystem** ($\mathfrak{A}$).

To structure this space of perspectives, Heim introduces the fundamental concept of **Metropie** ($g$) (SM, p. 13). Metropie functions as an intrinsic metric tensor that quantifies the *"Abstandsverhältnisse der einzelnen Aspekte des Systems zueinander"* [@heim2000] (the distance relationships between individual aspects of the system). The triple of the aspect system, its generator parameters, and its metric tensor constitutes an **Aspektivfeld**:

$$\mathfrak{A} \equiv \begin{pmatrix} \alpha ; S \\ p ; g \end{pmatrix}$$

Metropie is dynamic: metric modulators ($\gamma$ for discrete structural steps, $f$ for continuous shifts) govern the smooth or abrupt deformation of perspective geometry across the field of awareness.

## Kategorien ($\mathfrak{K}$), Syllogismen, and the Metrophor ($\mathfrak{M}$)

Heim models conceptual understanding not as an unstructured web of associations, but as hierarchically stratified **Kategorien** ($\mathfrak{K}$) (SM, pp. 14–19) [@heim2000]. A category is organized into distinct layers of **Syndrome** ($\mathfrak{S}_k$), stratified by their degree of conditionality (*Bedingtheit*):

* **Episyllogismus ($k \uparrow$):** The constructive, bottom-up synthesis of higher-order complex concepts from simpler, less conditioned constituents.
* **Prosyllogismus ($k \downarrow$):** The reductive, top-down analytical decomposition of complex concepts into their foundational constituents.

At the base of every category ($k=1$) lies an unconditioned, un-derived foundational layer termed the **Idee** ($\mathfrak{S}_1$). The Idee is populated exclusively by **apodiktische Elemente** ($a_i \in \mathfrak{M} = \mathbf{AP}_0$)—primitive, invariant concepts whose meaning remains absolutely stable across all aspect variations within that domain. The ordered collection of these apodictic elements forms the **Metrophor** ($\mathfrak{M}$), which serves as the immutable measure-bearer of the entire generative engine: the **Syntrix**.

## Funktoren, Quantoren, and Wahrheitsgrade: The Scaling of Invariance

Heim draws a rigorous distinction between variant and invariant structural statements (SM, pp. 20–23) [@heim2000]:

* **Funktoren ($\mathfrak{F}, \Phi$):** Aspect-dependent, non-apodictic propositions whose truth value fluctuates as the observer moves across different subjective aspects within an Aspektivfeld.
* **Quantoren:** Invariant relational laws that hold with necessity between Funktoren across a defined domain of aspects. A Quantor captures structural constancy amidst perceptual flux.
* **Wahrheitsgrad (Degree of Truth):** The precise scope of validity characterizing a Quantor:
  * *Monoquantor* ($Q_{\text{mono}}$): Valid within a single, isolated aspect system.
  * *Polyquantor (Discrete)* ($Q_{\text{poly}}^{(r)}$): Invariant across $r$ distinct, disjoint aspect systems $A_\rho$ (possessing a truth degree of $r$).
  * *Polyquantor (Continuous)* ($Q_{\text{poly}}^{(B)}$): Invariant across a continuous, path-connected manifold of aspect systems $B_\rho$.
  * *Universalquantor* ($U$): Invariant across the totality of all conceivable aspect systems.

The quest for a Universalquantor—a statement of absolute structural necessity holding across all possible observers—drives the entire transition from subjective aspect logic to the objective recursive machinery of the **Syntrix**.

## Intuitive Guide: What is a Structured Context?
To the reader, moving from Heim's philosophical discussion of subjective aspects to formal category theory can feel like crossing a linguistic abyss. Why formulate a subjective perspective as a Banach space? The intuitive leap is simple: think of a "context" not as an abstract mathematical gadget, but as a local scientific laboratory. Every observer operates with a finite set of measurement channels (our space $P^U$), a specific threshold of perceptual tolerance (the operator norms), and a framework of confidence or doubt (the dialectical space $D^U$). When two different observers compare notes, they are not comparing absolute truths; they are mapping how one laboratory's instruments translate into another's. The category $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ simply organizes these laboratories and their translation rules into a rigorous global landscape.

::: {#def-ban-le1}
### The Symmetric Monoidal Closed Category $\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}$
The symmetric monoidal closed category $(\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1},\widehat{\otimes}_\pi)$ [@ryan2002; @maclane1998] consists of:

1. **Objects:** Real Banach spaces $(X, \|\cdot\|_X)$.
2. **Morphisms:** Linear contractions $T \colon X \to Y$ with $\|T\|_{\text{op}} \coloneqq \sup_{\|x\|_X \le 1} \|T(x)\|_Y \le 1$.
3. **Monoidal Tensor Product:** The completed projective tensor product $X \widehat{\otimes}_\pi Y$ [@ryan2002], equipped with the projective cross-norm defined on general completed elements by:
   $$\|u\|_{X \widehat{\otimes}_\pi Y} \coloneqq \inf \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^\infty \|x_i\|_X \|y_i\|_Y \mathrel{\Bigg|} u = \sum_{i=1}^\infty x_i \otimes y_i,\; \sum_{i=1}^\infty \|x_i\|_X\|y_i\|_Y < \infty \right\}$$
4. **Internal Hom:** For $Y,Z\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1})$, the internal hom object is the Banach space $\mathcal{L}(Y, Z)$ equipped with the operator norm, yielding the natural contractive adjunction:
   $$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}}(X \widehat{\otimes}_\pi Y, Z) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}}\left(X, \mathcal{L}(Y, Z)\right)$$
   where hom-sets are the closed unit balls $\operatorname{Ball}(\mathcal{L}(X \widehat{\otimes}_\pi Y,Z))\cong\operatorname{Ball}(\mathcal{L}(X,\mathcal{L}(Y,Z)))$.
:::

## Vector Envelopes, Cones, and Isometric Ciphers

Let $\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{R}) \coloneqq \{ (a, f, b) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid a \le f \le b \}$ be the closed convex parameter space of bounded real intervals.

1. **Vector Envelopes:** Finite-dimensional real Hilbertian Banach spaces $P^U$ and $D^U$ equipped with the $\ell^2$-norm to ensure $\operatorname{Isom}(P^U) \cong \mathrm{O}(3n)$ is a continuous Lie group, equipped with fixed closed convex bounded state cylinders $\mathcal{S}_P^U \subset \operatorname{Ball}(P^U)$ and $\mathcal{S}_D^U \subset \operatorname{Ball}(D^U)$. For coordinate envelopes of rank $n$, $P_n \coloneqq \bigoplus_{\ell^2}^n \mathbb{R}^3 \cong (\mathbb{R}^{3n}, \|\cdot\|_2)$ with coordinate state cylinders $\mathcal{S}(P_n) \coloneqq \prod_{q=1}^n \mathcal{I}_{\le 1}(\mathbb{R})$, where $\mathcal{I}_{\le 1}(\mathbb{R}) \coloneqq \{ (a, f, b) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid -1 \le a \le f \le b \le 1 \}$.
2. **Isometric Ciphers:** Ciphers $z^U \in \operatorname{Isom}(P^U)$ and $\zeta^U \in \operatorname{Isom}(D^U)$ are linear isometric automorphisms on the local envelopes:
   $$\operatorname{Isom}(P^U) \coloneqq \{ T \in \mathcal{B}(P^U, P^U) \mid T \text{ is bijective, linear, and } \|T(x)\|_{P^U} = \|x\|_{P^U} \ \forall x \in P^U \}$$
   yielding evaluated spaces $P_{z^U} \coloneqq (P^U, \|\cdot\|_{z^U})$ and $D_{\zeta^U} \coloneqq (D^U, \|\cdot\|_{\zeta^U})$ with $\|p\|_{z^U} \coloneqq \|z^U(p)\|_{P^U}$.
3. **Channel Space:** A finite-dimensional Banach space $C^U$ (e.g., $C = \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\ell^\infty$-norm).

## Definition of $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ and Explicit Pullback Construction

::: {#def-ctx-adm-sk}
### The Uniform Hilbertian Category $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$
The category $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ is defined as a small skeleton category where:

1. **Objects:** Tuples $U = (D^U, P^U, C^U, K^U, z^U, \zeta^U, \mathcal{S}_D^U, \mathcal{S}_P^U)$, where $D^U, P^U, C^U$ are finite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces equipped with their canonical inner products $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_2$, closed convex bounded state spaces $\mathcal{S}_D^U \subset D^U, \mathcal{S}_P^U \subset P^U$, $z^U \in \mathrm{O}(P^U)$, $\zeta^U \in \mathrm{O}(D^U)$ are orthogonal isometries, and $K^U \colon D^U \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^U \to C^U$ is a linear contraction with $\|K^U\|_{\text{op}} \le 1$.
2. **Morphisms:** Triples of linear contractions $\alpha = (\alpha_D, \alpha_P, \alpha_C) \colon U \to V$ satisfying:
   $$\alpha_C \circ K^U = K^V \circ (\alpha_D \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \alpha_P), \quad \alpha_P \circ z^U = z^V \circ \alpha_P, \quad \alpha_D \circ \zeta^U = \zeta^V \circ \alpha_D$$
   and state preservation $\alpha_P(\mathcal{S}_P^U) \subseteq \mathcal{S}_P^V$, $\alpha_D(\mathcal{S}_D^U) \subseteq \mathcal{S}_D^V$.
:::

::: {#prop-functorial-projections}
### Functorial Projections and Natural Coordination
The construction of the category $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ induces three canonical projection functors and a governing natural transformation that formalize the internal structure of subjective perspectives:

1. **Projection Functors:** There exist three faithful functors $D, P, C \colon \mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}} \to \mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}$ mapping each context $U$ to its respective Banach spaces and each morphism $\alpha$ to its component contractions:
   $$F(U) \coloneqq F^U, \quad F(\alpha) \coloneqq \alpha_F \quad \text{for } F \in \{D, P, C\}$$
2. **Coordination Natural Transformation:** The family of linear contractions $K^U \colon D^U \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^U \to C^U$ constitutes a natural transformation:
   $$K \colon (D \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P) \Longrightarrow C$$
   where $D \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P$ is the composite functor defined by the monoidal product in $\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}$.
:::

::: {.proof}
For any morphism $\alpha \colon V \to U$ in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$, the Coordination Commutativity axiom requires:

$$\alpha_C \circ K^V = K^U \circ (\alpha_D \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \alpha_P)$$

Substituting the functorial definitions $C(\alpha) = \alpha_C$, $D(\alpha) = \alpha_D$, and $P(\alpha) = \alpha_P$, this equation becomes:

$$C(\alpha) \circ K^V = K^U \circ (D(\alpha) \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P(\alpha))$$

This is precisely the naturality square for $K$ with respect to the functor $D \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P$. Functoriality of $D, P, C$ follows from the componentwise composition defined in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$. $\blacksquare$
:::

::: {#prop-ctx-pullback}
### Explicit Construction of Pullbacks in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$
Let $\alpha \colon V_1 \to U$ and $\beta \colon V_2 \to U$ be morphisms in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$. Then the categorical pullback $W \coloneqq V_1 \times_U V_2$ exists in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ and is constructed as follows:

1. **Componentwise Fiber Products in $\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}$:**
   $$\begin{aligned}
   D^W &\coloneqq D^{V_1} \times_{D^U} D^{V_2} = \{ (d_1, d_2) \in D^{V_1} \oplus_\infty D^{V_2} \mid \alpha_D(d_1) = \beta_D(d_2) \} \\
   P^W &\coloneqq P^{V_1} \times_{P^U} P^{V_2} = \{ (p_1, p_2) \in P^{V_1} \oplus_\infty P^{V_2} \mid \alpha_P(p_1) = \beta_P(p_2) \} \\
   C^W &\coloneqq C^{V_1} \times_{C^U} C^{V_2} = \{ (c_1, c_2) \in C^{V_1} \oplus_\infty C^{V_2} \mid \alpha_C(c_1) = \beta_C(c_2) \}
   \end{aligned}$$
   Taking the pullback in the underlying finite-dimensional Banach category using the $\ell^\infty$ direct sum norm guarantees that the canonical projections $\pi_{1D}, \pi_{2D}, \pi_{1P}, \pi_{2P}, \pi_{1C}, \pi_{2C}$ are strict contractions with operator norm $\le 1$.
2. **Induced Coordination Contraction $K^W$:**
   We construct linear maps $K_1, K_2$ originating from the *same* domain $D^W \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^W$:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   K_1 &\coloneqq K^{V_1} \circ (\pi_{1D} \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \pi_{1P}) \colon D^W \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^W \longrightarrow C^{V_1} \\
   K_2 &\coloneqq K^{V_2} \circ (\pi_{2D} \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \pi_{2P}) \colon D^W \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^W \longrightarrow C^{V_2}
   \end{aligned}$$
   They satisfy the compatibility condition $\alpha_C \circ K_1 = \beta_C \circ K_2$ because:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   \alpha_C \circ K^{V_1} \circ (\pi_{1D} \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \pi_{1P}) &= K^U \circ (\alpha_D \pi_{1D} \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \alpha_P \pi_{1P}) \\
   &= K^U \circ (\beta_D \pi_{2D} \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \beta_P \pi_{2P}) = \beta_C \circ K^{V_2} \circ (\pi_{2D} \widehat{\otimes}_\pi \pi_{2P})
   \end{aligned}$$
   By the universal property of $C^W$ as a pullback in $\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}$, there exists a unique linear map $K^W \colon D^W \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^W \to C^W$ such that $\pi_{1C} \circ K^W = K_1$ and $\pi_{2C} \circ K^W = K_2$. Furthermore, $K^W$ is contractive: for any $u \in D^W \widehat{\otimes}_\pi P^W$ with $\|u\| \le 1$:
   $$\|K^W(u)\|_{C^W} = \max(\|K_1(u)\|_{C^{V_1}}, \|K_2(u)\|_{C^{V_2}}) \le \max(\|K^{V_1}\|_{\text{op}}, \|K^{V_2}\|_{\text{op}}) \|u\| \le 1$$
3. **Pullback Ciphers and Cones:**
   $\mathcal{S}_D^W \coloneqq (\pi_{1D})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_D^{V_1}) \cap (\pi_{2D})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_D^{V_2})$ and $\mathcal{S}_P^W \coloneqq (\pi_{1P})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_P^{V_1}) \cap (\pi_{2P})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_P^{V_2})$ are closed convex subsets. The product operator $z^W(p_1, p_2) \coloneqq (z^{V_1}(p_1), z^{V_2}(p_2))$ preserves the pullback subspace $P^W$ due to cipher naturality ($\alpha_P(z^{V_1}(p_1)) = z^U(\alpha_P(p_1)) = z^U(\beta_P(p_2)) = \beta_P(z^{V_2}(p_2))$), yielding $z^W \in \operatorname{Isom}(P^W)$. Analogously, $\zeta^W \in \operatorname{Isom}(D^W)$.
:::

::: {.proof}
Closed subspaces of finite-dimensional Banach spaces are complete. Given any test object $Y \in \mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ with morphisms $f_1 \colon Y \to V_1$ and $f_2 \colon Y \to V_2$ satisfying $\alpha \circ f_1 = \beta \circ f_2$, the unique linear contractions $u_D \colon D^Y \to D^W$, $u_P \colon P^Y \to P^W$, $u_C \colon C^Y \to C^W$ induced by the universal properties in $\mathbf{Ban}_{\le 1}$ assemble into a unique morphism $u = (u_D, u_P, u_C)$ in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ satisfying the pullback universal property. $\blacksquare$
:::

# Stratum A: Subobject Classifier $\Omega$, Internal Heyting Algebra, and Hyperdoctrines

::: {#thm-heyting-algebra}
### Complete Heyting Algebra Structure on $\Omega(U)$
For every context $U \in \mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$, $\Omega(U)$ is a complete Heyting algebra externally (and $\Omega$ is an internal Heyting algebra object) under:

$$\begin{aligned}
R_1 \wedge R_2 &\coloneqq R_1 \cap R_2 \\
\bigvee_{\lambda \in \Lambda} R_\lambda &\coloneqq \operatorname{cl}_{J_{\text{syn}}}\left( \bigcup_{\lambda \in \Lambda} R_\lambda \right) \\
R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2 &\coloneqq \{ f \colon V \to U \mid f^*(R_1) \subseteq f^*(R_2) \} \\
\neg_J R &\coloneqq (R \Rightarrow_J \bot) = \left\{ f \colon V \to U \mid f^*(R) \subseteq \operatorname{cl}_{J_{\text{syn}}}(\emptyset) \right\}
\end{aligned}$$

where $\bot(V) \coloneqq \operatorname{cl}_{J_{\text{syn}}}(\emptyset)$ acts as the minimal sieve. We explicitly require $J_{\text{syn}}$ to be a **subcanonical topology**, ensuring that all representable presheaves $\operatorname{Hom}(-, U)$ are strictly sheaves in $\mathcal{E}_{\text{syn}}$, preserving the faithfulness of context embeddings.
:::

::: {.proof}
* **Sieve Property:** Let $f \in (R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2)$ and $g \colon W \to V$. Then $(f \circ g)^*(R_1) = g^*(f^*(R_1)) \subseteq g^*(f^*(R_2)) = (f \circ g)^*(R_2)$, so $f \circ g \in (R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2)$.
* **$J_{\text{syn}}$-Closure:** Let $f \colon V \to U$ such that $f^*(R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2) \in J_{\text{syn}}(V)$. Let $h \in f^*(R_1)(W)$, so $f \circ h \in R_1$. Pulling back along $h$ gives $h^*(f^*(R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2)) \in J_{\text{syn}}(W)$. For any $q \in h^*(f^*(R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2))(Y)$, $(f \circ h \circ q) \in R_1$, so $(f \circ h \circ q) \in R_2$. Thus $h^*(f^*(R_2)) \in J_{\text{syn}}(W)$. Because $R_2$ is $J_{\text{syn}}$-closed, $f \circ h \in R_2$, proving $f^*(R_1) \subseteq f^*(R_2)$ and $f \in (R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2)$.
* **Heyting Adjunction:** $S \le (R_1 \Rightarrow_J R_2) \iff S \cap R_1 \subseteq R_2$ holds by construction [@maclane1994]. $\blacksquare$
:::

# Stratum B: Sheaf Descent, Descent Defects, and the Holoform Equalizer

::: {#def-holoform-equalizer}
### The Holoform Equalizer
Let $\mathbf{P} \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{syn}}$ be a sheaf on $(\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}, J_{\text{syn}})$. Presupposing that the relevant finite products and overlap pullbacks $V_i \times_U V_j$ exist in $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$, for an admissible cover $\mathcal{U} = \{\rho_i \colon V_i \to U\}_{i=1}^m$, the **Holoform Equalizer** is the categorical limit:

$$\mathsf{Hol}(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{P}) \coloneqq \operatorname{Eq}\left( \prod_{i=1}^m \mathbf{P}(V_i) \overset{d^0}{\underset{d^1}{\rightrightarrows}} \prod_{i, j=1}^m \mathbf{P}(V_i \times_U V_j) \right)$$

where $d^0(s)_{ij} \coloneqq \pi_{1, ij}^*(s_i)$ and $d^1(s)_{ij} \coloneqq \pi_{2, ij}^*(s_j)$ along the canonical pullback projection contractions $\pi_{1, ij} \colon V_i \times_U V_j \to V_i$ and $\pi_{2, ij} \colon V_i \times_U V_j \to V_j$ in $\mathbf{FinBan}_{\le 1}$.
:::

::: {#def-descent-defect}
### Descent Defect for Additive Presheaves
For an additive presheaf $\mathbf{A} \in \operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{PSh}(\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}))$, the **Descent Defect** of a local family $s = (s_i)_{i=1}^m \in \prod_{i=1}^m \mathbf{A}(V_i)$ is the Čech 0-coboundary:

$$\delta^0 \colon \check{C}^0(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{A}) \longrightarrow \check{C}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{A}), \qquad \operatorname{Def}_{\mathcal{U}}(s) \coloneqq d^0(s) - d^1(s) = \delta^0(s) \in \check{C}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{A})$$

where $\delta^0(s) = 0$ strictly denotes that $s$ is a Čech $0$-cocycle on the additive presheaf $\mathbf{A}$.
:::

::: {#thm-exact-descent-holoform}
### Exact Descent and Invariant Holoform Amalgamation
Let $\mathbf{P} \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{syn}}$ be a sheaf, and let $s = (s_i)_{i=1}^m$ be a candidate family of local sections over $\mathcal{U} = \{\rho_i \colon V_i \to U\}_{i=1}^m$ ($\mathsf{Stratum} = \mathbf{B}, \mathsf{Type} = \mathbf{M}, \mathsf{Role} = \text{Derivation}, \mathsf{Status} = \mathbf{S_2}, \mathsf{Evid} = \mathbf{E_4}^{\text{proof}}$).

1. The family $s$ satisfies the matching condition on overlaps if and only if $d^0(s) = d^1(s)$.
2. Strictly for additive presheaves $\mathbf{A} \in \operatorname{Ab}(\mathbf{PSh})$, the condition $d^0(s) = d^1(s)$ is equivalent to the vanishing of the Čech 0-coboundary $\operatorname{Def}_{\mathcal{U}}(s) \coloneqq \delta^0(s) = 0 \in \check{C}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{A})$.
3. When $d^0(s) = d^1(s)$, by the sheaf condition there exists a unique global section $\Gamma \in \mathbf{P}(U)$ and a canonical isomorphism $\theta_{\mathcal{U}} \colon \mathbf{P}(U) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathsf{Hol}(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{P})$ such that $\rho_i^*(\Gamma) = s_i$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, m\}$.
:::

# Stratum B: Typed Functional Mereology, Axiomatic CEM, and Relational Coordination

::: {#def-cem-envelope}
### The Internal Heyting Mereological Universe $\mathfrak{M}_{\text{asp}}$
The mereological universe over context $U$ is the complete Heyting subobject lattice:

$$\mathfrak{M}_{\text{asp}}(U) \coloneqq \operatorname{Sub}_{\mathcal{E}_{\text{syn}}}\left( \mathbf{y}(U) \times (\underline{\mathsf{Pred}}_U \sqcup \underline{\mathsf{Qual}}_U) \right)$$

where parthood is internal subobject inclusion $\operatorname{Part}(A, B) \iff A \le B$ in $\Omega(U)$, preserving constructive intuitionistic logic without forcing the Law of Excluded Middle.
:::

::: {#def-coordination-strength}
### Continuous $t$-Norm Coordination Strength
Let $\mathbf{z}_x \in [0, 1]^{|Q_{\text{pred}}|}$ and $\zeta_x \in [0, 1]^{|Q_{\text{qual}}|}$ be the contextual salience vectors of context $U = S_{\text{mod}}(x)$, let $\chi \colon [0, 1] \times [0, 1] \to [0, 1]$ be a continuous $t$-norm compatibility function, and let $\sigma_{dr} \colon [\alpha_r, \beta_r] \to [0, 1]$ and $\sigma_{fq} \colon [a_q, b_q] \to [0, 1]$ be the canonical affine interval normalizations:

$$\sigma_{dr}(v) \coloneqq \frac{v - \alpha_r}{\beta_r - \alpha_r}, \qquad \sigma_{fq}(v) \coloneqq \frac{v - a_q}{b_q - a_q}$$

1. The **Relational Coordination Strength** between qualifier $(d_r, v_{dr})$ and predicate $(f_q, v_{fq})$ is given explicitly by:
   $$\operatorname{Strength}\left( (d_r, f_q), \; S_{\text{mod}}(x) \right) \coloneqq \chi\left( (\zeta_x)_r \cdot \sigma_{dr}(v_{dr}), \; (\mathbf{z}_x)_q \cdot \sigma_{fq}(v_{fq}) \right) \in [0, 1]$$
2. A predicate-qualifier pair is **actively coordinated** in $S_{\text{mod}}(x)$ if and only if its relational strength exceeds the context threshold $\theta_{\text{coord}}(x)$:
   $$k_{\text{coord}} \in \mathsf{Coord}_U \iff \operatorname{Strength}\left( (d_r, f_q), \; S_{\text{mod}}(x) \right) > \theta_{\text{coord}}(x)$$
:::

::: {#prop-cem-compliance}
### Axiomatic Classical Extensional Mereology (CEM) Compliance
The subobject lattice $(\mathfrak{M}_{\text{asp}}(U), \le)$ satisfies the constructive CEM parthood axioms:

1. **Axiom MSL-M1 (Elemental Subobject Parthood):** For any predicate subobject $\mathbf{p} \le \underline{\mathsf{Pred}}_U$ and qualifier subobject $\mathbf{q} \le \underline{\mathsf{Qual}}_U$:
   $$\operatorname{Part}(\mathbf{p}, S_{\text{mod}}(x)) \iff \mathbf{p} \le S_{\text{mod}}(x) \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname{Part}(\mathbf{q}, S_{\text{mod}}(x)) \iff \mathbf{q} \le S_{\text{mod}}(x)$$
2. **Axiom MSL-M2 (Coordinated Parthood):** For the coordinated join $\mathbf{k}_{\text{coord}} = \mathbf{q} \vee \mathbf{p}$:
   $$\operatorname{Part}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{k}_{\text{coord}}) \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname{Part}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{k}_{\text{coord}})$$
3. **Guarded Subobject Join (Fusion):** For every family $\mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathfrak{M}_{\text{asp}}(U)$, the mereological fusion is the internal lattice join:
   $$\sigma\mathcal{X} \coloneqq \bigvee_{A \in \mathcal{X}} A \in \mathfrak{M}_{\text{asp}}(U).$$
:::

## Tripartite Antagonism Taxonomy and Synthesis via Aufhebung

::: {#def-taxonomy-antagonisms}
### Taxonomy of Antagonisms and Incompatibility
1. **Internal Incompatibility Predicate ($\operatorname{Incomp}_{\mathcal{E}}$):** For subsheaves $A,B \le \mathfrak{M}_{\text{asp}}(W)$, define the closed sieve:
   $$\operatorname{Incomp}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B) \coloneqq \llbracket |\operatorname{ev}_P(A)-\operatorname{ev}_P(B)|>\theta_{\text{tol}}\rrbracket \in \Omega(W).$$
2. **Mereological Antagonism ($\operatorname{Ant}_{\text{mer}}$):**
   $$\operatorname{Ant}_{\text{mer}}(S) \iff \exists A,B\le S\;\bigl(\operatorname{Part}(A,S)\wedge\operatorname{Part}(B,S)\wedge \operatorname{Incomp}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)=\top_{\Omega(W)}\bigr).$$
3. **Descent Antagonism ($\operatorname{Ant}_{\text{desc}}$):** Non-vanishing Čech 0-coboundary: $\operatorname{Def}_{\mathcal{U}}(s) \coloneqq d^0(s) - d^1(s) \ne 0 \in \check{C}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{A})$.
4. **Logical Antagonism ($\operatorname{Ant}_{\text{log}}$):** Internal contradiction of an adjoined theory: $W \Vdash (\phi \wedge \neg_J \phi) \iff W \Vdash \bot$.
:::

::: {#thm-antagonism-separation}
### Descent Discrepancy vs. Subcanonical Non-Vanishing
Let $\mathcal{U} = \{V_1, V_2\}$ be an admissible cover of $U$ with overlap $W = V_1 \times_U V_2$. An evaluative discrepancy $|v_1 - v_2| > \theta_{\text{tol}}$ induces a non-vanishing Čech descent defect ($\operatorname{Ant}_{\text{desc}}$), preventing global section amalgamation in $\mathsf{Hol}(\mathcal{U}, \mathbf{A})$. The overlap context $W$ remains a valid non-zero object ($\mathbf{y}W \not\cong \mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{E}}$), preserving subcanonicity and the Yoneda embedding.
:::

::: {.proof}
1. $\operatorname{Def}_{\mathcal{U}}(s)_{12} = s_1|_W(f_q) - s_2|_W(f_q) = v_1 - v_2 \ne 0$ since $|v_1 - v_2| > \theta_{\text{tol}} \ge 0$. In $S(W)$, both $(f_q, v_1)$ and $(f_q, v_2)$ are subsets, satisfying $\operatorname{Incomp}_{\text{eval}}$.
2. By the translation schema, $p_{v_1} \vdash \neg p_{v_2}$. On $W$, both local evaluations are forced, so $W \Vdash p_{v_1} \wedge p_{v_2} \implies W \Vdash p_{v_1} \wedge \neg p_{v_1}$, which implies $W \Vdash \bot$ in the Heyting algebra $\Omega(W)$. $\blacksquare$
:::

::: {#thm-synthesis-aufhebung}
### Reflexive Abstraction as Synthesis via Aufhebung
Let $S_{\text{mod}}(x)$ contain an antagonism $\operatorname{Incomp}(A, B)$. The process of **Reflexive Abstraktion** models dialectical sublation (*Aufhebung*) as an operator mapping $S_{\text{mod}}(x)$ to a successor context $S'_{\text{mod}}(x')$ containing a synthesized composite whole $C \coloneqq \operatorname{Synthesize}(A, B)$ such that:

1. $\operatorname{Part}(C, S'_{\text{mod}}(x'))$ holds.
2. $C$ is internally consistent with respect to the initial incompatibility:
   $$\neg \exists A', B' \left( \operatorname{Part}(A', C) \wedge \operatorname{Part}(B', C) \wedge \operatorname{Incomp}(A', B') \right)$$
3. The synthesized whole $C$ acts as an initial generator for higher-level syndrome construction in $\operatorname{Prop}_1$ of the Syntrix hierarchy.
:::

## Layer 2 Frame Extraction and the Extended Pseudometric

::: {#def-extended-pseudometric}
### Total Context Discrepancy Extended Pseudometric $d_P$
For $U,V \in \operatorname{Obj}(\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}})$, define the extended distance by mutual deformation retractions:

$$d_P(U,V) \coloneqq \inf_{\substack{\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}(U,V)\\\beta\in\operatorname{Hom}(V,U)}}\max_{F\in\{D,P,C\}}\max\left(\|I_{F^U}-\beta_F\alpha_F\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|I_{F^V}-\alpha_F\beta_F\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right),$$

with $\inf\emptyset\coloneqq\infty$.
:::

::: {#prop-extended-pseudometric}
### Global Extended Pseudometric Verification
$d_P$ is an extended pseudometric on $\mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$ satisfying:

1. **Reflexive Zero:** $d_P(U, U) = 0$ (taking $\alpha = \beta = \operatorname{id}_U$).
2. **Symmetry:** $d_P(U, V) = d_P(V, U)$ by symmetry of the $\max$ operation.
3. **Extended Triangle Inequality:** For any $U, V, W \in \mathbf{Ctx}_{\text{adm}}^{\text{sk}}$, $d_P(U, W) \le d_P(U, V) + d_P(V, W)$.
:::

::: {.proof}
If $d_P(U, V) = \infty$ or $d_P(V, W) = \infty$, the inequality holds trivially. If both are finite, for any $\varepsilon > 0$, choose contractions $(\alpha_1, \beta_1)$ between $U, V$ and $(\alpha_2, \beta_2)$ between $V, W$ such that their maximum distances are bounded within $d_P(U, V) + \varepsilon/2$ and $d_P(V, W) + \varepsilon/2$ respectively. Composing $(\alpha_2 \alpha_1, \beta_1 \beta_2)$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\|I_{P^U} - \beta_1 \beta_2 \alpha_2 \alpha_1\|_{\text{op}} &= \|I - \beta_1 \alpha_1 + \beta_1(I - \beta_2 \alpha_2)\alpha_1\|_{\text{op}} \\
&\le \|I - \beta_1 \alpha_1\|_{\text{op}} + \|\beta_1\|_{\text{op}} \|I - \beta_2 \alpha_2\|_{\text{op}} \|\alpha_1\|_{\text{op}}
\end{aligned}$$

Since $\|\alpha_1\|_{\text{op}} \le 1$ and $\|\beta_1\|_{\text{op}} \le 1$, this is $\le \|I - \beta_1 \alpha_1\|_{\text{op}} + \|I - \beta_2 \alpha_2\|_{\text{op}} < d_P(U, V) + d_P(V, W) + \varepsilon$. Symmetry bounds $\|I_{P^W} - \alpha_2 \alpha_1 \beta_1 \beta_2\|_{\text{op}}$ identically. Taking $\varepsilon \to 0$ proves $d_P(U, W) \le d_P(U, V) + d_P(V, W)$. $\blacksquare$
:::

2. The **Infinitesimal Metropie Metric Tensor** is:
   $$g_{\text{met}}(\dot{z}_1, \dot{z}_2) \coloneqq -\frac{1}{2} \operatorname{tr}\left( (z^{-1}\dot{z}_1) \circ (z^{-1}\dot{z}_2) \right) \ge 0$$

# Stratum B: Fischer--Servi Intuitionistic Modal Syntrometric Logic ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{iMSL}}$)

## Well-Founded Inductive Syntax

::: {#def-msl-terms}
### Generative Terms $\operatorname{Gen}_k$ and Immediate Generative Parthood $\operatorname{IGP}$
* **Base Generation ($\operatorname{Gen}_0$):** $\operatorname{Gen}_0 \coloneqq \mathbf{AP}_S \sqcup \mathbf{AP}_0$, where $\mathbf{AP}_S = \{p_0, p_1, \dots\}$ and $\mathbf{AP}_0 = \{a_0, a_1, \dots, a_N\}$.
* **Inductive Syndrome Generation ($\operatorname{Gen}_{j+1}$):**
  $$\operatorname{Gen}_{j+1} \coloneqq \{ \operatorname{Conj}(\phi, \psi), \, \operatorname{Lift}_{\Box_{\text{Syn}}}(\phi), \, \operatorname{ParaConj}(\phi) \mid \phi, \psi \in \operatorname{Gen}_j \}$$
* **Immediate Generative Parthood ($\operatorname{IGP}_{j+1} \subset \mathbf{Prop}_j \times \operatorname{Gen}_{j+1}$):**
  $$\operatorname{IGP}_{j+1}(X, P') \iff (P' = \operatorname{Conj}(X, Y)) \vee (P' = \operatorname{Conj}(Y, X)) \vee (P' = \operatorname{Lift}_{\Box_{\text{Syn}}}(X)) \vee (P' = \operatorname{ParaConj}(X))$$
* **Cumulative Generative Base:** $\mathbf{Prop}_k \coloneqq \bigcup_{j=0}^k \operatorname{Gen}_j$.
:::

::: {#def-msl-wff}
### The Stratified Language $\mathbf{WFF}_{\text{iMSL}}$ and Assertion Levels $\mathcal{L}_k$
The full language $\mathbf{WFF}_{\text{iMSL}}$ is defined inductively by the grammar:

$$\phi, \psi \Coloneqq P \mid \bot \mid (\phi \wedge \psi) \mid (\phi \vee \psi) \mid (\phi \to \psi) \mid \operatorname{Stable}_k(P) \mid \Box_{\text{Syn}} P \mid \Box_S \phi \mid \Diamond_S \phi \mid [\pi_F]\phi \mid \langle\pi_F\rangle\phi$$

where $P \in \bigcup_{j \ge 0} \mathbf{Prop}_j$. The tree depth $\operatorname{deg}(\phi) \in \mathbb{N}$ provides a well-founded induction measure. The **assertion level** $\mathcal{L}_k \subset \mathbf{WFF}_{\text{iMSL}}$ consists of formulas whose atomic propositions belong to $\mathbf{Prop}_k$ and whose stability predicates $\operatorname{Stable}_j(P)$ and $\Box_{\text{Syn}} P$ satisfy $j \le k$.
:::

::: {#def-truth-conditions-imsl}
### Exact Intuitionistic Truth Conditions
For world $w = (U, k)$ and $\phi \in \mathcal{L}_k$:

$$\begin{aligned}
(U, k) \models P &\iff \nu_U(P) = \top_{\Omega(U)} \quad (\text{for } P \in \mathbf{Prop}_k) \\
(U, k) &\not\models \bot \\
(U, k) \models \phi \wedge \psi &\iff (U, k) \models \phi \text{ and } (U, k) \models \psi \\
(U, k) \models \phi \vee \psi &\iff (U, k) \models \phi \text{ or } (U, k) \models \psi \\
(U, k) \models \phi \to \psi &\iff \forall (U', k) \succeq (U, k), \; (U', k) \models \phi \implies (U', k) \models \psi \\
(U, k) \models \Box_S \phi &\iff \forall (U_{\text{ref}}, k) \succeq (U, k), \; \forall (U', k) \left( (U_{\text{ref}}, k) R_{\Box_S} (U', k) \implies (U', k) \models \phi \right) \\
(U, k) \models \Diamond_S \phi &\iff \exists (U', k) \text{ s.t. } (U, k) R_{\Diamond_S} (U', k) \text{ and } (U', k) \models \phi \\
(U, k) \models \operatorname{Stable}_0(a_i) &\iff (U, k) \models a_i \quad (\text{for } a_i \in \mathbf{AP}_0) \\
(U, k) \models \operatorname{Stable}_0(\operatorname{Coord}(d, p)) &\iff (U, k) \models \operatorname{Coord}(d, p) \wedge \operatorname{Strength}((d, p), U) > \theta_{\text{coord}}(U) \\

(U, k) \models [\pi_F]\theta &\iff (F(U), k+1) \models \theta \\
(U, k) \models \langle\pi_F\rangle\theta &\iff (F(U), k+1) \models \theta
\end{aligned}$$

# Stratum B: Metatheory --- Soundness and Constructive Canonical Completeness

::: {#thm-soundness-reduction}
### Soundness of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{iMSL}}$
The Leveled Sequent Calculus of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{iMSL}}$ is sound with respect to the Leveled Kripke Semantics:

$$S(x); \Gamma \vdash^k \phi \implies S(x); \Gamma \models^k \phi$$
:::

::: {.proof}
By induction on the derivation height of $S(x); \Gamma \vdash^k \phi$:

1. **Axioms:** $(\text{Ax})$ and $(\text{Fact-S}_A)$ are valid by definition. $(K_{\pi_F})$ and $([\pi_F] D \langle\pi_F\rangle)$ are sound because $R_{\pi_F}$ is a single-valued, deterministic total function on worlds.
2. **Intuitionistic Rules:** Soundness of $(\to R)$ and $(\to L)$ follows from the persistence preorder $\preceq$ and Proposition @prop-valuation-persistence.
3. **Modal $\mathbf{KTB}$ Rules:** $(\Box_S\text{-Ref})$ is sound because $d_P(U, U) = 0 < \epsilon_A$ implies reflexivity. The $B$-rules hold because $d_P(U, V) = d_P(V, U)$ implies symmetry. Confluence holds by the Fischer--Servi path restriction [@fischerservi1984].
4. **Stability & Dynamic Rules:** $(\operatorname{Stab}_0 R)$ and $(\operatorname{Stab}_{j+1} R)$ match the hereditary recursive clauses of Definition @def-truth-conditions-imsl. Soundness of $([\pi_F]\Box_{\text{Syn}}\text{Prop})$ follows from condition (d) of $R_{\pi_F}$. $\blacksquare$
:::

::: {#def-prime-theories}
### Leveled Prime Saturated Theories
Let $k \in \mathbb{N}_0$. A set of formulas $\Delta \subset \mathcal{L}_k$ is a **Prime Saturated $k$-Theory** relative to context $S(x)$ if:

1. **Deductive Closure:** $\forall \psi \in \mathcal{L}_k, \; (S(x); \Delta \vdash^k \psi) \implies \psi \in \Delta$.
2. **Consistency:** $S(x); \Delta \nvdash^k \bot$ (hence $\bot \notin \Delta$).
3. **Primeness (Disjunction Property):** $\forall \psi_1, \psi_2 \in \mathcal{L}_k, \; (\psi_1 \vee \psi_2 \in \Delta) \implies (\psi_1 \in \Delta \lor \psi_2 \in \Delta)$.
:::

::: {#lem-prime-extension}
### Leveled Prime Extension Lemma
If $S(x); \Gamma \nvdash^k \phi$, there exists a Prime Saturated $k$-Theory $\Delta \subset \mathcal{L}_k$ such that $\Gamma \subseteq \Delta$ and $\phi \notin \Delta$.
:::

::: {.proof}
Enumerate the countable formulas of $\mathcal{L}_k$ as $\psi_0, \psi_1, \dots$. Define the inductive sequence $\Delta_0 \coloneqq \Gamma$, and:

$$\Delta_{n+1} \coloneqq \begin{cases}
\Delta_n \cup \{\psi_n\} & \text{if } S(x); \Delta_n, \psi_n \nvdash^k \phi \text{ and } \psi_n \text{ is not a disjunction}, \\
\Delta_n \cup \{\psi_n, \alpha_i\} & \text{if } S(x); \Delta_n, \psi_n \nvdash^k \phi \text{ and } \psi_n = \alpha_1 \vee \alpha_2 \text{ (where } i \in \{1, 2\} \text{ avoids deriving } \phi\text{)}, \\
\Delta_n & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}$$

Setting $\Delta \coloneqq \bigcup_{n=0}^\infty \Delta_n$ yields a prime saturated $k$-theory containing $\Gamma$ with $\phi \notin \Delta$. $\blacksquare$
:::

::: {#thm-simultaneous-saturation}
### Simultaneous Multi-Level Prime Saturation Theorem
Let $S(x); \Gamma_0 \nvdash^0 \phi_0$ be an unprovable root sequent at level $k=0$. There exists an infinite sequence of Prime Saturated Theories $\mathbf{\Delta} \coloneqq \langle \Delta_k \subset \mathcal{L}_k \rangle_{k \in \mathbb{N}_0}$ such that:

1. $\Gamma_0 \subseteq \Delta_0$ and $\phi_0 \notin \Delta_0$.
2. For every $k \in \mathbb{N}_0$, $(\Delta_k, k) R_{\pi_F}^c (\Delta_{k+1}, k+1)$, meaning:
   $$\{ \theta \in \mathcal{L}_{k+1} \mid [\pi_F]\theta \in \Delta_k \} \subseteq \Delta_{k+1} \quad \text{and} \quad F_{\text{ops}}(\Delta_k \cap \mathbf{Prop}_k) \subseteq \Delta_{k+1}$$
3. The canonical evaluation satisfies the full Truth Lemma across all levels $k \in \mathbb{N}_0$:
   $$(\Delta_k, k) \models \psi \iff \psi \in \Delta_k \quad \forall \psi \in \mathcal{L}_k$$
:::

::: {.proof}
Extend $\Gamma_0$ to prime 0-theory $\Delta_0$ with $\phi_0 \notin \Delta_0$ via Lemma @lem-prime-extension. Inductively, given $\Delta_k$, define $\Theta_{k+1} \coloneqq \{ \theta \mid [\pi_F]\theta \in \Delta_k \} \cup F_{\text{ops}}(\Delta_k \cap \mathbf{Prop}_k)$. If $\Theta_{k+1} \vdash^{k+1} \bot$, rule $([\pi_F]\text{-Unstep})$ forces $\Delta_k \vdash^k [\pi_F]\bot \to \bot$, contradicting consistency of $\Delta_k$. Thus $\Theta_{k+1}$ extends to a prime $(k+1)$-theory $\Delta_{k+1}$. Truth Lemma induction holds globally on the lexicographic rank $\mathbf{m}(\psi) = (\operatorname{rk}(\psi), k)$. $\blacksquare$
:::

::: {#thm-strong-completeness-imsl}
### Strong Completeness of Leveled Kripke Models for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{iMSL}}$
If a sequent $S(x); \Gamma_G \models^k \phi$ is semantically valid (where $\Gamma_G \subset \mathcal{L}_k$ contains global axioms and $\phi \in \mathcal{L}_k$), then $S(x); \Gamma_G \vdash^k \phi$ is derivable in the $\mathcal{L}_{\text{iMSL}}$ sequent calculus.
:::

::: {.proof}
1. **Canonical Frame Definition:** Let $W^c \coloneqq \{ (\Delta', k') \mid k' \in \mathbb{N}_0, \, \Delta' \text{ is a Prime Saturated } k'\text{-Theory} \}$. Define canonical relations:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   ((\Delta_1, k_1), (\Delta_2, k_2)) \in \preceq^c &\iff (k_1 = k_2) \wedge (\Delta_1 \subseteq \Delta_2) \\
   ((\Delta_1, k'), (\Delta_2, k')) \in R_{\Box_S}^c &\iff \{ \psi \in \mathcal{L}_{k'} \mid \Box_S \psi \in \Delta_1 \} \subseteq \Delta_2 \\
   ((\Delta_1, k'), (\Delta_2, k'+1)) \in R_{\pi_F}^c &\iff \{ \theta \in \mathcal{L}_{k'+1} \mid [\pi_F]\theta \in \Delta_1 \} \subseteq \Delta_2 \wedge F_{\text{ops}}(\Delta_1 \cap \mathbf{Prop}_{k'}) \subseteq \Delta_2
   \end{aligned}$$
   and valuation $V^c((\Delta', k'), P) \coloneqq \text{True} \iff P \in \Delta'$.
2. **Existence of a Prime-Saturated $F$-Successor:** For any Prime Saturated $k$-Theory $\Delta_1$, the set $\Theta \coloneqq \{ \theta \in \mathcal{L}_{k+1} \mid [\pi_F]\theta \in \Delta_1 \} \cup F_{\text{ops}}(\Delta_1 \cap \mathbf{Prop}_k)$ is $(k+1)$-consistent by soundness of $([\pi_F]\text{-Step})$ and $(\langle\pi_F\rangle FI)$. Hence the prime extension lemma supplies a Prime Saturated $(k+1)$-Theory $\Delta_2$ with $((\Delta_1, k), (\Delta_2, k+1)) \in R_{\pi_F}^c$.
3. **The Truth Lemma:** We prove by structural induction on $\psi \in \mathcal{L}_{k'}$ that for all $w^c = (\Delta', k') \in W^c$:
   $$w^c \models \psi \iff \psi \in \Delta'$$
   * *Atoms:* $w^c \models P \iff P \in \Delta'$ holds by definition of $V^c$.
   * *Connectives:* Follows from the maximality and deductive closure of $\Delta'$.
   * *Modal $\Box_S \psi$:* If $\Box_S \psi \in \Delta'$, then for all $w'^c \in R_{\Box_S}^c(w^c)$, $\psi \in \Delta'' \implies w'^c \models \psi$, so $w^c \models \Box_S \psi$. Conversely, if $\Box_S \psi \notin \Delta'$, the set $\{\chi \mid \Box_S \chi \in \Delta'\} \cup \{\neg \psi\}$ is $k'$-consistent, extending to a witnessing Prime Saturated $k'$-Theory $\Delta''$ with $w'^c \nvDash \psi$, so $w^c \nvDash \Box_S \psi$.
   * *Stability $\operatorname{Stable}_{j+1}(P')$:* Follows from inductive closure under $(\operatorname{Stab}_{j+1} R)$ and $(\operatorname{Stab} L_1), (\operatorname{Stab} L_2)$.
   * *Dynamic $[\pi_F]\theta$:* $w^c \models [\pi_F]\theta \iff w_{\text{succ}}^c \models \theta \iff \theta \in \Delta_{\text{succ}} \iff [\pi_F]\theta \in \Delta'$.
4. **Countermodel:** The canonical world $w_\Delta^c = (\Delta, k)$ satisfies $w_\Delta^c \models \Gamma_G$ and $w_\Delta^c \nvDash \phi$. By contraposition, $S(x); \Gamma_G \models^k \phi \implies S(x); \Gamma_G \vdash^k \phi$. $\blacksquare$
:::
